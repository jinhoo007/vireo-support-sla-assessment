# Memo: Weekly First-Response SLA Review

**To:** Neha Kulkarni, Support Operations Manager  
**Subject:** Weekly first-response SLA review — where attention is needed

## What we found

We reviewed **11,200 unique support tickets** using the current first-response rules.

- **2,440 tickets missed the required first-response time.**
- This is a **21.79% breach rate**.
- Each missed response results in a **₹350 store credit**.
- The total related credit exposure is therefore **₹854,000**.

The largest concentration is in the **Morning shift**:

- **2,018 breaches** occurred during the Morning shift.
- This represents **82.70% of all breaches**.
- The Morning shift had a **32.22% breach rate**.

This means the Morning shift is the clearest place to start the review. It does not, by itself, tell us why the breaches happened.

## Where to focus

The report provides both the number of breaches and the breach rate for each agent and shift. This is important because an agent handling more tickets can naturally have more breaches even when their rate is not the highest.

The agent-level report should therefore be used to identify specific cases for review rather than to label people as underperforming.

A small review of ticket messages also showed repeated mentions of **delivery/shipping issues** and **technical troubleshooting**. These are useful areas to investigate, but the sample is too small to say that they caused the missed response times.

## Recommended actions for Monday

**1. Review the Morning shift first.**  
Look at where the response delays are occurring at the start of the shift and during the busiest periods.

**2. Review high-volume and high-rate cases separately.**  
Use the weekly agent report to discuss individual cases with the relevant team members, while considering how many tickets each person handled.

**3. Review the delayed tickets for common operational problems.**  
Pay particular attention to delivery-related requests and tickets requiring technical troubleshooting. The purpose is to understand what is slowing the first response, not to assign blame.

## What this report does not say

The analysis does **not** conclude that the Morning shift or any particular issue caused the breaches. It shows where the breaches are concentrated and provides a practical starting point for the next review.

It also does not recommend adding staff. The immediate opportunity is to understand where the existing process is losing response time and address the most concentrated problems first.

**Bottom line:**  
The current baseline is **21.79% of tickets missing the first-response target, representing ₹854,000 in automatic store-credit exposure**. The **Morning shift accounts for 82.70% of all observed breaches**, making it the clearest starting point for the next operational review.
